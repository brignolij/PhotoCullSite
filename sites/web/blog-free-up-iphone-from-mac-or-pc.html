How to free up your iPhone from a Mac or Windows PC

Jeffrey Brignoli · November 16, 2026

Using an Android phone? Read the Android guide: How to free up your Android phone's storage from a Mac or PC

Cleaning a full iPhone on the iPhone is tedious. From a computer you can see far more photos at once and decide faster. Here is the whole process.

What you need

  • A Mac (Apple Silicon, macOS 11+) or a Windows 10/11 PC.
  • A USB cable. On Windows, also the free Apple Devices app from the Microsoft Store.
  • PixCuller, free for both systems.
  • For deleting from the iPhone: iCloud Photos turned off and, on a Mac, macOS 27 or later. On macOS 26 or earlier, PixCuller still shows and sorts the photos, and you delete them in the iPhone's Photos app.

Step by step

1. Plug in and trust. Connect the iPhone, unlock it and tap Trust.

2. Open the Trips view. PixCuller groups your camera roll by trip and shows the space each takes.

3. Sort the heaviest trips. Compare similar shots, keep the best, and mark the rest, plus screenshots and big videos you don't need.

4. Confirm the deletion. With iCloud Photos off (and macOS 27 or later on a Mac), PixCuller deletes the photos directly over the USB cable, on Windows too. The deletion is immediate and permanent on the iPhone, but by default PixCuller keeps a copy of each deleted item on your computer for 30 days, in a “PixCuller Trash” folder in Pictures.

5. If iCloud Photos is on. PixCuller refuses the deletion: turn iCloud Photos off (Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Photos) or delete in the iPhone's Photos app.

Is it safe?

Yes: your photos stay on your devices, with no account, no cloud and no upload. PixCuller only reads your photos until you confirm. A deletion is permanent on the iPhone, but a copy stays on your computer for 30 days by default.

How many photos can I delete per day?

As many as you want: there is no daily limit and nothing to buy. PixCuller is free, with no subscription.

On Windows

The steps are the same on Windows 10 and 11. Install the free Apple Devices app from the Microsoft Store first, so PixCuller can read the iPhone. The app is not code-signed yet, so SmartScreen may warn on first launch.
